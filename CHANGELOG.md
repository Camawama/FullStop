# FullStop! Changelog

## Unreleased

### Added
- **Landing on a mob, a boat or a minecart rides it** (round 6, 2026-10-17; new Entity Riding section: `enableEntityRiding` on, `entityRidingMobs` / `entityRidingBoats` / `entityRidingMinecarts` on, `entityRidingMinSpeed` 3 m/s, `entityRidingMaxSpeed` 30, `entityRidingCrouchPrevents` on, `entityRidingMobRiders` off, `entityRidingMountPanic` on, `entityRidingDismountCooldownTicks` 40). The owner: "The old one had the ability to jump onto an entity to ride it. The new one feels a lot more polished though. I want to make sure we didn't lose any features so I'd like the entity riding to be re-implemented for the new one, and also it could be its own config options." The old experimental collisions (`entityCollisionDamage`) mounted a player on whatever mob, boat or cart it came down on; the new Entity Collisions crushed it. Now a player that comes down on top of one that nothing rides yet, at a landing's speed (3 to 30 m/s: a jump lands at 6-8, a 10-block fall at 14), mounts it instead of colliding: no crush, no damage, no push, and a mob landed on panics and bolts with its rider on its back (the vanilla panic goal, slowed by the ride weight as before). The old rules are kept (`EntityRiding`): never while crouching (the way to drop onto a mob without riding it), never a creative flyer or a spectator, never a body already riding, never onto something ridden or crouching, never heavier than a living mount (a boat or a cart takes anyone: a 75 kg player on a 60 kg boat), never onto one's own kind unless a baby, never within the dismount cooldown (now a setting; during it the rider and its last mount don't collide either), never a ring of riders. Added: never onto a player, never a body another mod holds outright (a physgun's grip, an MKW race: Camafy's `heldOutrightByOther`, so Full Stop now needs Camafy 0.1.23 or newer, the pack's is 0.1.42), only rideable minecarts (a chest cart was mountable before), and `entityRidingMobRiders` lets mobs ride what they land on (the old collisions mounted players only; on, every living body is looked at from `entityRidingMinSpeed` instead of half the collision speed, a little more server time among many mobs). Faster landings are the crush they were. Game tests `EntityRidingTests` (8): a fake player moved straight down at a steady speed as its client would: onto a cow at 8 m/s it rides it, the cow unhurt and panicking at its rider; crouching, nothing happens at all (8 m/s is under the collision speed); at 40 m/s the cow is crushed, not mounted (a 75 kg player takes 4.6 m/s off a 650 kg cow, under the damage threshold); onto a chicken at 12 m/s it is too heavy to ride it and crushes it (300 m/s lost at the chicken's size, dead); onto a boat at 8 m/s it rides it. A cow falling onto a standing player at 15.9 m/s crushes it and rides nothing; a pig falling onto a cow (13.5 m/s) crushes, never mounts, by default; with `entityRidingMobRiders` on it rides the cow, put off it and dropped straight back it mounts nothing within the 40-tick cooldown, and rides again after. All 15 `MidAirCollisionTests` and `FishSchoolTests` still pass with the old path gone.
- **Create's flywheel is dangerous; a spinning shaft carries what stands on it** (round 15, 2026-10-13; Create section: `flywheelHazards` on, `shaftSurfaceGrip` 0.6; damage type `fullstop:flywheel`, "was flung by a flywheel"). The owner: "it appears the flywheel from create isn't dangerous at all. Additionally, standing on a spinning shaft should push the player or entity in the direction it's turning." A flywheel's rim (1.37 blocks from its axis, past its own block) is a single gear's teeth at the rim's speed, by the wheel's own spin with Camafy's real flywheels (a coasting wheel turns on): pushed off along the rim, bitten (`gearTouchDamage` at 7.5 m/s, at most `gearMaxDamage`), flung from `gearFlingSpeed`; it never forms a nip with a gear. A bare shaft (not encased) under a body on the ground draws the body's speed across it toward its top surface's (`omega x 3/16`, 5 m/s at 256 RPM) by `shaftSurfaceGrip` a tick, on the side that moves the body (a player's own client). Gametests `FlywheelShaftHazardTests`: a pig at a flywheel's rim at 16 RPM bitten 0.6 and never thrown, at 64 bitten 2.4 and never thrown, at 256 flung at 47.7 m/s; a pig on a shaft carried 0.70 blocks across it in a second at 64 RPM, 1.04 at 256, none on a still one (all 12 older kinetic tests still pass).
- **Only pointy things need a shallow angle to bounce off slime; fire charges bounce too** (round 15, 2026-10-13; `projectilesBounceOnSlime`; new tag `#fullstop:pointy_projectiles`: `#minecraft:arrows`, the trident). The owner: "the shallow angle for tridents and arrows are because they are "pointy" ... Ender pearls, eggs, etc should bounce at any angle", and "Fireballs projectiles (fire charge) should bounce off slime too". An arrow or trident (any `AbstractArrow`, or anything in the new tag) still skips only at up to `projectileSlimeSkipAngle` (30) and sticks when shot more steeply in; snowballs, eggs, potions, ender pearls, fire charges and ghast fireballs (added to `#fullstop:bounces_on_slime`) now bounce at any angle, straight down included, with the same restitution, friction and `projectileSlimeMaxSkips`. A bounce is no hit: a fire charge sets nothing alight and a ghast's fireball doesn't explode until it really lands, and its thrust (which pushes it on every tick) is mirrored in the face with its velocity, or it would turn straight back into the slime. Game tests `SlimeThrownTests` (14 cases): a snowball and a potion at 60 degrees and an egg and a pearl at 80 bounce and stay whole the tick after; a fire charge at 45 bounces away; an arrow skips at 12 and sticks at 60.
- **Jumping on a moving ship no longer walks you across the deck** (`relandingTakesDeckMotionOut`, Ship Riders, on). The owner: "If I stand on a slanted Valkyrien Skies ship and jump in place, my player will slowly slide upwards on the slant instead of just jumping in place." On a ship at rest nothing moves (harness `vs2_slope`: 0.000 blocks per jump on decks tilted 0 to 45 degrees, static, resting on the ground or held, on block middles and seams, single jumps and the jump key held, both packs); on a moving one it did: a jump hands the rider the deck's motion (`releaseLeavingRiders`), and landing back on the SAME deck Valkyrien Skies starts carrying it again without taking that motion back out (it does that only for a body landing on another ship than the one it stood on last), so every landing walked the rider forward across the deck. Now the deck's motion at the feet is taken out of a rider landing back on the deck it jumped off, as VS does for a new one. Jump key held 10 s on a deck tilted 30 degrees moving at 1 m/s: crept 1.1 blocks across it and slid 8.1 blocks off it down the slope -> 0.3 across, 0.03 down, still on it; flat deck 3.7 -> 0.9 blocks; ten single jumps on the 30-degree deck 1.06 -> 0.20. Rotors unchanged (a jump off a 32 RPM rotor flies straight, 0 ticks still carried).
- **Snowballs, eggs, potions and ender pearls skip off slime** (tag `#fullstop:bounces_on_slime`, same rules as arrows: `projectilesBounceOnSlime`, `projectileSlimeSkipAngle` 30, restitution 0.6, friction 0.8, at most 6 skips). The owner: "Snowballs, eggs, potions, and pearls should also bounce on slime at a shallow angle like arrows and tridents." A thrown item meeting a slime face (the world's or a ship's) at 30 degrees or less skips, and a skip is no hit (its impact event is cancelled): nothing shatters, splashes, hatches or teleports there, only where it really lands; steeper, slower than 3 m/s or past its 6th skip it lands as on any block. Pack harness `fs2_throw` (the player's own clicks, the pack without Better Toss): snowballs, eggs and pearls thrown level or 10-20 degrees down met the slime at 12-26 degrees, skipped 6 times (29 -> 12 m/s) and landed 19-36 blocks past the first skip, the pearl taking its thrower there and nowhere before; thrown 45 degrees down each broke at once; on a ship's slime deck a snowball skipped 6 times. Game tests `SlimeThrownTests` 7/7 (before: every one broke at once). A vanilla splash potion is a slow lob (10 m/s, tossed 20 degrees up) and comes down too steeply to skip unless thrown hard; with Better Toss a quick click throws at a third of the speed (too steep), a charged throw skips.
- **Arrows and tridents skip off slime; firework rockets bounce off it** (`projectilesBounceOnSlime`, new Bounces and booms section, on; `projectileSlimeSkipAngle` 30, `projectileSlimeRestitution` 0.6, `projectileSlimeFriction` 0.8, `projectileSlimeMaxSkips` 6; tag `#fullstop:bounces_on_slime`: arrows, spectral arrows, tridents, firework rockets). The owner: "If I shoot an arrow straight level with the face of a slime block, it should stick into the slime block. If I shoot an arrow at a shallow angle (just like our water skipping mechanic), the arrow should be able to bounce off ... with some dampening". Vanilla gives slime's bounce only to what lands on it through a move, and only straight up: an arrow ray-casts its own path and sticks into whatever it meets, and a rocket puts its velocity back after its move. Now an arrow or trident meeting slime (in the world or on a Valkyrien Skies ship, against the face's own motion) at up to 30 degrees above the face skips off it, the speed into the face coming back at 0.6 and the speed along it kept at 0.8, at most six times; steeper, slower than 3 m/s or past its sixth skip it sticks as on any block. Both sides decide alike (an arrow is simulated on its watchers' clients too). A skipped arrow is no longer critical. Firework rockets bounce off slime at any angle (their block hits were already Full Stop's: they skim instead of exploding on contact). Other thrown things (snowballs, eggs, potions, pearls) still broke on slime, as on anything they hit (since 2026-10-12 they skip too: see above). A `fullstop:physics_projectiles` projectile keeps Full Stop's full physics. Pack harness `fse_slime` (45 m/s arrows at a slime floor): launched 8, 15 and 22 degrees down they skip six times, slower each time (34.7, 25.8, 25.5, 19.8, 19.6 m/s), and stick 24 to 49 blocks on; at 28 degrees (meeting the floor at 30.2 after its fall) and steeper they stick at once, straight down too; a trident at 15 degrees skips six times (23 to 12 m/s), at 70 sticks; on a ship's slime deck an arrow at 15 degrees skips twice and flies off the deck's end, at 60 sticks. A rocket at 30 m/s into a slime wall comes back at 24 m/s, one launched up into a slime ceiling comes down (12 to -14.7 m/s); stone walls and ceilings bounce nothing. Both sides kept the same arrows in the same places (0.0 blocks apart).
- **Dropped slime balls and slime blocks bounce** (`bouncyItemRestitution` 0.8, tag `#fullstop:bouncy_items`: slime balls, slime blocks, `#forge:slimeballs`). The owner: "Dropping slime block or slime ball items should be bouncy." Such an item gives back 0.8 of its speed into whatever its move ran into (the ground, a ceiling, a wall), until it is slower than 1.6 m/s; it settles after a few bounces. Other items land as before (and still bounce on slime blocks as vanilla has them). Harness `fse_slime`: a slime ball and a slime block dropped 6 blocks onto stone bounce to 2.29, 0.99, 0.46, 0.16 and 0.04 blocks and lie still (an item resting on the ground falls a little every fourth tick: that is not a landing); cobblestone doesn't bounce.
- **Running into foliage never hurts** (`foliageNeverHurts`, on; tags `#fullstop:foliage`, `#fullstop:not_foliage`). The owner: "mods that add foliage shouldn't damage me when running into them, like the Huge Clover Petal from Biomes o' plenty". Biomes o' Plenty's petal is a two-pixel plate at the top of its block that a runner meets edge-on, and the stop was billed like a wall's. A block is foliage by what it is, not by a list of mods: it breaks at once or nearly (hardness up to 0.2, leaves') and is a plant by vanilla's own tags (leaves, flowers, saplings, crops, what a sword cuts, what a tree grows over), by its sound (grass, leaves, petals, dripleaf, moss, vines, roots...) or by a plant's map colour with a plant's manners (destroyed by a piston, replaceable, burnt by lava). Cactus, melons, pumpkins, hay and mushroom blocks are not. An impact only on foliage deals nothing unless it is a landing, and smashing through foliage (Full Stop breaks soft blocks a fast body runs into) costs nothing either; a landing on foliage counts as a soft one (as dripleaves and moss, x0.7). Harness `fse_foliage`, the survival player thrown at a wall at about 17 m/s: stone hurts (8.9 and 2.3), a moss wall and azalea leaves nothing (before: 3.9 and 8.0, from smashing through them), and in the main pack a row of Biomes o' Plenty's huge clover petals stops the player unhurt.
- **A slime bounce at an angle keeps its sideways speed** (`slimeBounceKeepsMomentum`, on). The owner: bouncing off a tilted ship's slime "there is far too much horizontal dampening from the bounce (maybe from entity drag?)". It is the drag: a living body in the air keeps 0.91 of its sideways motion a tick and 0.98 of its vertical (vanilla's air control for walking), so the sideways part of a bounce off a leaning slime face was a third gone in four ticks while its upward part carried on. The tick after the bounce took a fifth more: the body still counts as standing on the slime then, and its travel took the slime's ground friction (0.8 x 0.91). From such a bounce until the body touches anything, lands in water or flies, the sideways speed the bounce gave it decays as its vertical speed does (`SlimeFlight`, around the body's travel, the ground friction of that first tick included); its own air control is untouched. Harness `fse_tilt`, the player dropped 8 blocks onto a ship's slime deck held tilted 30 degrees (20.5 m/s in; the ideal reflection 17.8 m/s sideways): before, 16.2 m/s sideways after the bounce, 50% of it left five ticks later and 31% after ten, 6.3 blocks sideways in a second; now 17.4 m/s, 88% and 77%, 13.6 blocks. A pig (the server's bounce): 7.4 blocks before, 12.3 now.
- **Sonic booms carry far, loud and low-passed** (`sonicBoomRange` 768, was a fixed 192). The owner: "the sound should be heard from even further away but low passed the further away it is. It should still be loud from further away though." A boom is now sent to its listener as its own packet and played from where its shock wave came from with no distance falloff of the sound engine's own (vanilla's linear falloff made a boom 300 blocks off a whisper and nothing past 384): full to 48 blocks, still 45% at the edge of the range, deeper and low-passed by the distance it travelled through Camafy's `AudioEffects` (a crack close by, a thump far off). A supersonic rotor's train of cracks keeps its 192-block range. A Valkyrien Skies rotor's cracks now come from the point of its tips' ring nearest each listener, as a Create arm's already do (they came from the ship's centre). Network protocol 11. Pack harness `fse_sonic` (a test speed of sound of 100 m/s): a 25-block iron rotor at Mach 2 heard from 18 blocks off its hub, past its tips: the cracks come from 6.9 blocks away (the tips' ring, 12.5 out; before: from the hub, 18.3); a pig flown past at 120 m/s: the boom 12 blocks off at full loudness and unfiltered, and 400 blocks off (before: not heard past 192) at 0.69 of it with a high-frequency gain of 0.21.

- **Create contraptions meet each other** (`contraptionsMeet`, Create section, on). The owner: "if I have two create contraptions spinning next to each other and they collide, they don't actually collide with one another and break each other." A contraption block running into another contraption's block (two arms on bearings side by side, an arm and a parked cart's load) is judged by the terrain rules with both sides driven: the hit's energy is both blocks' (each contraption's effective mass behind its block at their closing speed), the weaker block is knocked off its contraption (both when about as tough, `mutualBreakRatio`), and when neither gives, or they meet slower than `contraptionTerrainMinSpeed`, both jam against each other, each stalled with its face against the other and its network overstressed until either drive stops or turns away (`ContraptionStress.Obstacle.contraption`: the jam holds while the other contraption's block is there and hasn't moved off). A pair is judged once per contact whichever of the two finds it; a train's carriages (Create judges trains itself) and coupled minecarts' loads are never met against each other. Game tests (`ContraptionMeetTests`, `-Pcreate`, 5 of 5): an iron arm at 64 RPM knocks the wool tip off an arm turning at 1 RPM beside it (32.6 m/s, A loses nothing, nobody jams); the same on a parked minecart contraption's wool; an iron arm at 2 RPM jams against a parked cart's load, held at 30.3 degrees, its tip 0.02 into it, let go when its motor stops.
- **The air bar is a reserve** (`blackoutOnlyWithoutAir`, client settings, on). The owner: "I should be able to lose all my bubbles and only after I lose my bubbles do I start to lose consciousness." Running out of air used to dim the screen and muffle sound with the bubbles ((1 - air/max)^3: 12% dark at half, black at the last bubble). Now nothing dims until the last two bubbles, which take consciousness a quarter of the way down with the air (the owner: "it should start when there are two bubbles left ... ramp up quicker when you have no bubbles left"); with the bar empty it fades faster, out cold 5 s later (vanilla's drowning damage ticks meanwhile), and comes back over 1.5 s after a breath (`client/render/OxygenBlackout`, read by the overlay and the muffle alike, timed on the player's ticks). Off: the old curve. G-force blackouts are unchanged.
- **Speed blows a fire out** (`windPutsOutFire`, on; `fireWindMinSpeed` 4.5 m/s, `fireWindDrain` 0.5, `fireWindMaxDrain` 10; Kinetic Damage settings). The owner: "if an entity is on fire, the fire can be put out by how fast they move. It should work with vs ships too. The flame shouldn't go out instantly, it should diminish." A burning body moving through the air loses its fire time faster the faster it goes, on top of vanilla's tick: nothing up to a walk, then 0.5 fire ticks a tick for every m/s over 4.5, at most 10 (`server/physics/interaction/WindOnFire`; the speed is the body's own step through the world, so riding counts and a teleport doesn't). Never in lava or standing in fire, never on a fire-immune body; blown out, it hisses and smokes. Eight seconds of fire last 8 s at a walk, 3.6 s at 7 m/s, 1.45 s at 14 m/s, 0.8 s at 30 m/s and faster (harness `fsb_fire`: 160 / 72 / 29 / 16 ticks, the client stops drawing the flames within a tick or two of the server). A burning Valkyrien Skies ship (Camafy's ship fire) under way goes out the same way, by its speed or, turning, half its far end's (`ShipFireCompat`: Camafy 0.1.33's `ShipFire.reduceFireTicks`, by name; with an older Camafy it is counted here and put out at the end): a raft burning 15 s at rest burned 3.5 s at 11 m/s.
- **Gears push ships and cars** (`gearsPushShips`, `gearsPushVehicles`, on; `gearShipMass` 1000 kg; Create section). The owner: "The gears from fullstop that apply knockback to an entity should apply knockback to valkyrien skies ships as well, and automobility vehicles." The same teeth and rules as for bodies (`compat/create/kinetic/GearPushables`): a single gear's teeth push a ship block that touches them and fling it from `gearFlingSpeed`, as an impulse where they touch (a hull caught off its middle turns), scaled by `gearShipMass` over the ship's mass; a ship block in the in-running nip of two meshing gears is drawn in and jams them with `gearJamStress` for the whole ship's mass. Gears on a ship act on other ships, never their own. Vehicles (`#fullstop:gear_pushed`: Automobility's and RIAutomobility's cars) are pushed, flung and jam a nip by their weight (a 1000 kg car a quarter of a player's push), moved only through Camafy's `Impulses` on the side that drives them (the driver's own client for a driven car, `GearVehiclesClient`). Harness `fsb_gears`: a one-plank ship (500 kg) pushed 2.1 blocks in 3 s at 64 RPM, flung at 8.9 m/s at 256 RPM; a nine-plank ship (4.5 t) 0.14 blocks; a plank in a nip loads it 250 SU/RPM.
- **TNT goes off on impact** (`tntImpacts`, on; `tntImpactMinSpeed` 5 m/s, `tntImpactInstantSpeed` 12 m/s, `tntImpactFuseTicks` 10; Ship Collisions). The owner: "TNT that is on a vs ship or is a vs ship should explode on impact with the terrain or another ship or create contraption". New `ImpactExplosives`; game tests `ShipCrushTests` (a TNT ship dropped 14 blocks: gone the tick it lands; 4 blocks: lit, the bang ten ticks later; TNT on the ground under a falling hull; a TNT ship rammed; the switch off) and `ContraptionTntTests` (`-Pcreate`: a bearing arm into TNT, an arm with a TNT tip into stone); pack harness `fss_tnt`.
- **A fist nudges a ship** (`punchShips`, on; `punchShipImpulse` 250 N·s; Ship Collisions). The owner: "hitting a block with your fist should apply a tiny bit of force to it so it moves". None while sneaking, with a tool or any other item in hand, in creative or spectator, on the ship the player stands on or rides, on a static ship, where the player may not alter blocks (adventure mode, Camafy's `MatchPolicies`), and at most once per 8 ticks per player (`ShipPunches`). Pack harness `fsm_punch` (the attack key on plank ships floating in water, the peak horizontal speed in the 15 ticks after): a one-block ship (500 kg) 0.40 m/s, a 3x3 raft (4.5 t) 0.045 m/s, a 9x9 raft (40.5 t) 0.0045 m/s (250 N·s would give 0.5 / 0.056 / 0.0062 without the water); a sneaking punch and a pickaxe's hit pushed nothing (0.008 and 0.004 m/s, the drift before). - **Create contraptions break the sound barrier** (`contraptionSonicBooms`, Create section, on). A moving contraption whose fastest point passes the speed of sound booms as a Valkyrien Skies hull does (Full Stop's `SonicBooms`: one boom per supersonic stretch for everyone its shock reaches, when it reaches them, from where it came; its riders, seated on it or standing on it, hear none), and a bearing's arm whose tips stay supersonic makes a supersonic rotor's shock train (one crack per supersonic tip per turn, its riders at the hub hearing it too). - **Create Catastrophes: contraptions meet the terrain and ships, both sides giving** (`contraptionTerrainImpacts`, `contraptionBlocksBreak`, `contraptionImpactStress`, `contraptionShipImpacts`, all on; `contraptionTerrainMinSpeed` 5 m/s, `contraptionImpactStrength` 4, `contraptionStressPerImpact` 64 SU, `maxContraptionTerrainChecksPerTick` 8192). Each server tick a moving contraption's outer blocks are followed through this tick's and the coming tick's motion in Create's own pose (the coming one because Create stops a sliding contraption - a gantry, piston or pulley - before it enters a solid block, so what it is about to run into is the contact; a bearing's arm, which Create lets pass through blocks, is met a tick early). A block whose centre enters a solid world cell or a Valkyrien Skies ship's block at `contraptionTerrainMinSpeed` or faster has hit it; slower, contraptions pass through blocks as in Create; rails, Create's tracks and the block the contraption is driven from (a retracting piston's poles slide back into it) are never hit. Which side gives is FullStop's ship material rule (toughness = hardness x (mass / 1000 kg)^massInfluence, the weaker gives, within `mutualBreakRatio` both): an iron arm carves stone; a wool sail hitting stone is knocked off the moving contraption (through Create's own block-change packet, so every client redraws it, its colliders rebuilt; its drops fly on with the contraption's speed; never the anchor, a block with a block entity, a seat, an actor or a control, never the last block). The hit's energy is the contraption's kinetic energy behind the striking block (its whole mass for one that slides or rides, its moment of inertia over the block's distance from the axis squared for one on a bearing) times `terrainEnergyFraction` and `contraptionImpactStrength`; a hit short of breaking cracks the block (FullStop's own cracks for crackable blocks, remembered here for the rest) and jams the contraption against it. The contraption feels it through Create's own stress: every hit loads the block driving it (bearing, piston, pulley, gantry shaft) with `contraptionStressPerImpact` SU per energy unit, fading by a tenth a tick, added where Create sums a network's stress (`calculateStressApplied`), so a stressometer shows it and too much overstresses the network; a hit nothing gave way to - bedrock, a block too tough for this hit, a protected block, a ship far heavier than the contraption or a static one - jams it: it stops with its block's face against the obstacle (a bearing is set to the angle where they meet; Create stops a piston or pulley there itself) and is stalled the way Create stalls a contraption whose drill is still mining, so it holds still on every client too, and its network is loaded with twice its capacity, so it is overstressed and everything on it stops. It stays so, grinding nothing, for as long as its drive still turns it into the obstacle and the obstacle is there: stopping the motor, reversing it, or mining or replacing the block lets go, and the load is dropped at once. Started again into the same block it hits it afresh at the new speed. A bearing whose motor stops sets its arm down at the last quarter turn it passed (Create rounds to the nearest one, which would put the arm through the obstacle and break it, or drop the arm's block at bedrock). A gantry takes itself apart as soon as its shaft stops (Create's own gantry stop), so it is set down as blocks right in front of the obstacle. Minecart contraptions and trains have no network: they lose the hit's energy from their motion and stop dead on a jam. A contraption meeting a ship pushes it at the contact as an immovable driven body would (the ship's effective mass there, capped as every FullStop push) and breaks the weaker block (the ship's by `hullDamage`). Minigame hosts and break events can refuse every break. F3+V: paths turn red, and stop, where they run into the terrain or a ship at hit speed, and a red path is drawn from each outer block about to strike. Game tests (`-Pcreate`, `ContraptionTerrainTests`): an iron arm at 64 RPM breaks stone in one pass (2,123 SU peak on its network, still turning); into bedrock it jams (overstressed, stopped) and turns again the tick after the bedrock is removed; a wool arm loses its tip to stone, which stands (5 -> 4 blocks, the wool dropped, still turning); an arm at 8 RPM passes stone untouched; a gantry with an iron ram ploughs through dirt at 9.8 m/s; a minecart contraption at 8 m/s stops dead at bedrock (a furnace cart, 4 m/s at most, passes through); off, nothing. - **Create Catastrophes: gears and water wheels** (`gearHazards`, `waterWheelHazards`, Create section, on; idle without Create). Gears crush only where two of them mesh: a body at the in-running side of the nip of two meshing cogwheels (two side by side on one axis, or a large and a small one diagonally: Create's own meshing rule), where both gears' teeth run into the mesh, is drawn in (`gearPullStrength`, growing with the square root of the speed; a sneaking player braces, heavier bodies are harder to drag) and crushed whenever vanilla's damage cooldown allows: `gearDamage` (3) at 64 RPM, growing with the speed to the 0.75 power, a quarter more with a large cogwheel in the pair, nothing below 8 RPM (`fullstop:gear`, "was caught in the gears"; `fullstop:large_gear`, "was ground up by a large cogwheel"). Only a body at the nip's mouth is caught: its nearest point within the smaller gear's radius (plus a tenth of a block) upstream of the mesh, between the two rims. The body jams the gears too: it loads their network with `gearJamStress` (0.5) stress units per RPM per kilogram, as Create counts a machine's impact (a player: 37.5 SU/RPM, more than a lone water wheel or a hand crank supplies; a pig 75, a cow 325; a creative motor never notices), so a big body or a weak network overstresses and Create stops it; stopped, the gears neither draw in nor bite, the body stays wedged, and the load goes as soon as it walks out (or dies). The out-running side pushes out. A single spinning gear never pulls and never hurts: a body touching its teeth is pushed off, out from the gear and along the way the teeth move, a nudge from a crawling gear, a shove of up to 7 m/s at 256 RPM. Encased cogwheels, gearboxes and shafts are safe; saws, drills, crushing wheels, fans and belts already act on their own. Two large cogwheels meshing at a right angle have no nip (yet). Water wheels (both sizes) sweep a body off their top and down into the water, and on the downstroke and round the bottom every paddle presses it under: it can't surface (a swimming stroke up is cancelled) and drowns by vanilla's own air and drowning, unless it swims out sideways along the axle (the wheel holds weakly that way), the wheel stops, or it has been held `waterWheelHoldSeconds` (30), after which it is let go for five seconds; on the upstroke the paddles carry it up and out. A wheel driven at 24 RPM or more also bites (half a gear nip's bite) out of the water. `gearMinRpm` (4): anything slower catches nothing. How: a mob is moved by the server, a player by their own client (which owns a player's movement), both finding the parts the same way (block states and Create's synced speed); damage, spits and the grinding sound are the server's. A body held is claimed (Camafy MOTION and G_FORCE, so other movement mods yield and the tug is no crash to black out from); a body another mod claims, or one the gravity mod holds to a surface, isn't pulled; creative and spectator players are never caught. Parts on Valkyrien Skies ships (Clockwork) work in the ship's pose, scaled with it and moving with it. Never hit: anything riding the ship, spectators and creative players, entities tagged `fullstop:ship_immune`, blacklisted entities, and bodies another mod owns the impacts of (MKW's karts and their drivers in a race). New and teleported ships get a grace period (`newShipGraceTicks`), so assembling, pasting or chunk-loading a ship never counts as a crash.
- **Ships break terrain - and themselves.** Ships crashing into terrain break what they hit, weighed by material: every block has a toughness, its hardness times Valkyrien Skies' own mass for it (`hardness * (mass / 1000 kg) ^ massInfluence`: leaves 0.04, grass 0.75, oak planks 1.0, stone 3.75, deepslate 8.7, obsidian 250), and at each contact only the WEAKER of the terrain block and the ship's own block there can give. A stone hull crushes grass; a wooden hull rammed into stone loses planks at the contact while the stone holds (`hullDamage`, on by default; hardness alone would have ranked planks above stone); obsidian against obsidian breaks nothing. Breaking the weaker block costs `toughness * breakCostMultiplier * r / (r - 1)` (r = the toughness ratio), so near-equal materials take an enormous crash and equal ones never break; the ship's kinetic energy into the terrain pays, fastest contact first, and nothing breaks below `breakMinSpeed` (5 m/s). Works on every Valkyrien Skies physics backend (`physicsBackend` in the world's `vs-core-server.toml`): on PhysX and Jolt the crashes come from VS's collision events, on the default KRUNCH_CLASSIC (which sends none) from Camafy's collision fallback (Camafy 0.1.2+). Only contacts that just began count the ship's speed from the tick before, so a hull resting, sliding or rolling on the ground never grinds itself away. Blocks drop like mined ones (a hull's drops land in the world), ice cracks accumulate as for entities, and when every terrain block the ship hit gave way it keeps going with the energy left - if anything held, or its hull gave way, it stays stopped. Ships hitting ships break nothing unless `shipVsShipBreaking` is FRAGILE or FULL (same toughness rule). Unpiloted ships follow mobGriefing; every break asks protection mods (break event) and minigame hosts. Blocks in `fullstop:ship_unbreakable` (default: `#minecraft:wither_immune`), slime and honey never break. Needs Camafy's ship-collision bridge.
- **Ships plough through vegetation.** Valkyrien Skies lets ships pass straight through leaves; now a ship moving at `sweepMinSpeed` (4 m/s) or faster breaks the leaves and soft plants its hull passes through (new block tag `fullstop:ship_sweepable`: leaves, saplings, flowers, tall grass, ferns, vines, sugar cane, berry bushes...), with drops and a little drag (`sweepDrag`); drifting slower, it slides through untouched. Leaves players placed stay unless `sweepPlacedLeaves` is on; crops, water plants and waterlogged blocks are never swept.
- **Ship impact sounds.** A ship hitting terrain or another ship plays both blocks' own sounds at the contact (a stone cube landing on grass: grass and stone), louder and higher-pitched the faster the hit (a heavier ship a little louder and deeper), with block-crack particles on harder hits (`impactSounds`, `impactSoundMinSpeed`, `impactSoundVolume`, `impactParticles`). Only new contacts sound, so resting and sliding ships stay quiet; it works with block breaking switched off.
- Vanilla Backport compatibility (optional tag entries - nothing changes without it): its sulfur cubes and happy ghasts are `fullstop:kinetic_immune` like slimes and ghasts (a sulfur cube no longer dies from its own bounces), pale moss blocks cushion like moss, and its bushes, wildflowers, dry grass, leaf litter, pale hanging moss/moss carpet and resin clumps are `fullstop:ship_sweepable`.
- `/fullstop weigh <targets>`, `/fullstop weigh held` and `/fullstop ship info` (permission level 2). WeightRS's `/weightrs weigh` and `/weightrs held` stay as aliases while WeightRS isn't installed.

- The g-force effect thresholds are SERVER settings now (`minGForceThreshold`, `maxGForceThreshold` under General settings); they gate a gameplay mechanic - blackouts, nausea, the Clarity and Vertigo potions that shift them - so a client must not be able to move them. Existing client config values are ignored.
- Dizziness: sustained spinning makes a player nauseous. The turning rate - the larger of the view's yaw rate and the heading rate of a fast run - is averaged over a second, and only turning faster than `spinNauseaThreshold` (15 degrees a tick) builds up dizziness, one degree per degree beyond that rate; it drains the same way as soon as you slow down, and nausea starts at `spinNauseaTurns` (2) full turns of it. A quick 180, a fast 360, a minute of hard mouse-look or running in circles never make you dizzy; spinning at 30 degrees a tick does after about 4 s, at 20 after about 9.5 s, and it lifts about 3 s after you stop. Spinning never blacks out: that stays reserved for linear g. Clarity/Vertigo scale the threshold; 0 disables it.
- The two g-force effects are now distinct and layered: past `maxGForceThreshold` (the screen fully black) the player is BLINDED - a G-LOC that outlasts the g itself - with nausea that lingers well after the blindness fades; halfway between the two thresholds nausea alone sets in. Before, both were hardcoded (5 and 8) and independent of the vignette's own thresholds.
- `minimumFallDistance` (Kinetic Damage settings): the vertical damage threshold as a fall height in blocks. Falls (and any impact along gravity) up to that height never hurt; above 0 it replaces `velocityDamageThresholdVertical` (3.0 blocks = 12.3 m/s, a little under the 12.77 m/s default; 4.0 = 14.2 m/s, 5.0 = 15.9 m/s). This is the same knob other movement mods call "min fall distance"; FullStop cancels vanilla fall damage outright, so theirs never applies while FullStop is installed and there is nothing to conflict with.

- Gravity Unbound compatibility (`enableGravityUnboundCompat`, on by default when the mod is installed). FullStop's whole damage model splits VERTICAL (falls: the 12.77 m/s threshold, boots, Feather Falling, sprains) from HORIZONTAL (wall runs: the 10.5 m/s threshold), and corroborates each with the matching collision flag. Both were read in world axes, and Gravity Unbound reports its flags in the entity's own rotated frame while a field-driven slam lands along whatever world axis the field points down — a fall onto a plated wall read as a "horizontal" impact with only "vertical" evidence, and the damage gate silently dropped it, so being pulled into things by a gravity field never hurt. Every vertical/horizontal split now goes through the entity's gravity frame (read once per tick from the mod's API and cached on the capability): the stopping-force decomposition, the mostly-downward classification, the grounded-drift and jump rules, the felt-gravity baseline, the raycast classifier's floor/wall/ceiling heuristics (whose ray starts are laid out in the tilted body's own frame — the stored bounding box is only its world-aligned envelope there), the flush-stop wall evidence, water skips and the bounce camera. Velocity is exchanged with the mod in WORLD space everywhere FullStop reads or writes `deltaMovement` (the client's velocity report, bounces, water skips, block-smash slowdowns, mount momentum, entity collisions), since the mod keeps that field in the entity's local frame. A world without the mod computes bit-identically to before.

- `debugImpactLog` (Kinetic Damage settings, off by default): one server-log line per significant impact tick with everything the damage gate looked at — the entity and its gravity frame, the pre-impact speed, the speed lost (in the frame and per world axis), the collision evidence (recent vertical/horizontal hit, onGround, ship block), immunity, what the rays hit, and the damage computed and applied. For reporting impacts that should have hurt but did not.

### Changed

- **Modpack compatibility: physics ownership.** FullStop now honours Camafy's physics-ownership contract (`PhysicsOwnership`): a mod that scripts an entity's motion claims the aspects it takes over, and FullStop leaves exactly those alone - crash damage, impact sounds and the passenger share (IMPACT), g-force effects, vignette, FOV and muffle (G_FORCE), slime/bed bounces, water skips and the bounce camera (BOUNCE), entity collisions, collision damage and velocity-scaled attack damage (CONTACT), block breaking, cracking, door/button triggering and phasing (BLOCKS), mount momentum and projectile momentum (MOTION), pressure, air loss, water drag and the drowning muffle (ENVIRONMENT). An entity claimed outright isn't simulated at all. With MKW Mobility this means karts and the players driving them get none of FullStop's crash damage, g-force effects, bounces, pushes or block breaking; a player who falls off a kart is back under FullStop (from a clean slate: the hand-over is treated like a teleport). Moves other mods mark as discontinuities (a gravity flip, a snap onto a rail) never read as velocity - including ones made on a player's own client (network protocol bumped to 4).
- Hits by something someone steers are that someone's attack: when a boat, horse, cart or fired projectile rams an entity, the driver or owner is now the damage source's attacker (the boat is still what struck). PvP, team and minigame rules and kill credit apply to them; death messages read "was hit by Steve's Boat".
- Kinetic block breaking asks the rest of the pack first: a crash nobody steers goes through Forge's mob-griefing event (not the raw gamerule), every broken block posts a break event (with the responsible player, or a "[Full Stop]" fake player) that claim/protection mods can cancel, and minigame hosts' no-change regions are respected (Camafy `MatchPolicies`). Dripstone snapping and falling-dripstone breaks follow the same rules.
- `fullstop:pressure` and `fullstop:atmosphere` are tagged `#minecraft:is_drowning`, so the `drowningDamage` gamerule and damage-type rules in other mods cover them.
- The g-force blackout is drawn without the depth test and after other HUD layers (LOW priority): it covers the whole screen, a telescope glare from Space Events included.
- FullStop's vanilla-push veto (ex-rider and ex-vehicle during the dismount grace) runs before Gravity Unbound's push rewrite (mixin priority 900), so it holds in every gravity frame.
- Camafy 0.1.3 is required (the KRUNCH_CLASSIC collision fallback of 0.1.2, and 0.1.3's `Gravity.isAnchored`).
- **A ship hit launches the entity even when it kills it**, so the body flies off the hull instead of dropping at it (a dead body is never hit again).
- **Players are launched at their real speed.** Vanilla's motion packet clamps each axis to 3.9 blocks a tick (78 m/s), so a player hit by an 80 m/s ship left at 78 m/s at most. Launches past that go to the player's client in FullStop's own vehicle-motion packet (and the clamped vanilla copy is dropped); slower ones stay vanilla's. A player hit by an 80 m/s ship now flies at about 1.3 × 80 = 104 m/s (`entityRestitution` 0.3), and a dying player's death camera follows the flying body.

### Fixed

- **Ships faster than 40 m/s hit nothing.** Valkyrien Skies simulates on its own clock and the game tick picks up its newest frame, so a tick can bring no new frame and the next one two. FullStop's ship tracker took the missing travel of such a tick for a teleport above 40 m/s and kept the ship in its 10-tick "jumped" grace over and over, where it hits nothing: a 60 m/s ship flew straight through a villager. The tracker now works in physics frames: a tick without a new frame changes nothing, "last tick's" motion is the previous frame's, each frame knows how much time it spans (game time, real time or the time its motion took, whichever is longest), and a jump is a frame whose centre of mass left the path its velocity allows over that span (at least half a second of it, for bursts of physics catching up). Ship hits also look ahead as far as the next frame can carry the hull and back through what it crossed in the last one, and a hull block only in that swept part meets the entity with its leading face: an 80 m/s ship's 4 m of travel per tick had it counted as a side-on graze. Measured with real ships on all three backends: 36, 60, 80, 120 and 200 m/s ships all hit and launch a villager at the launch rule's speed to within 0.1%, with no grace.
- **A mob standing on open ground hit by a ship is launched, not crushed.** The pin check looked for something solid a quarter block along the push, and a push with any downward component - float noise of the ship's rotation (a normal of 1e-14 downward was measured), or a ship pitched slightly nose-down - sank that probe into the block the mob stood on, so it was "pinned" (x1.5 damage, no launch). For an entity standing on the ground (in its gravity frame) the probe now drops the part that goes into the ground and looks the full distance along it. A wall or another ship behind it still pins it, and a hull coming down on it from above (a push more than about 45 degrees into the ground) still crushes it against the ground.

- A passenger of a player-driven vehicle (boat passengers, a horse's rider) no longer gets g-force blindness and nausea from a smooth ride. The rider was measured once per server tick while the vehicle moves once per client move packet, so the vehicle's doubled/empty packet ticks read as 2x speed then a dead stop. Passengers now replay their vehicle's per-client-tick motion.
- A non-downward impact over 5 damage no longer throws a rider off when the hit didn't actually land (cancelled by another mod or fully absorbed).
- `fullstop:physics_blacklist` is honoured everywhere: blacklisted vehicles get no mount-momentum seeds or driven-vehicle bounces (client and server), blacklisted passengers take no share of their vehicle's impact, blacklisted entities are never rammed in entity collisions, and they get no water drag, pressure or block-phasing drag.

- Repeated normal jumps no longer eventually deal impact damage, and flying straight with an elytra no longer builds up to a blackout. Both came from the same measurement flaw: a player's server-side position only changes when a move packet is applied, and the client's ticks are not phase-locked to the server's - whenever the two clocks drifted into alignment one server tick saw TWO move packets (a doubled position delta) and the next saw NONE (a dead stop). Measured per server tick, a routine jump landing read as an 18 m/s stop (above the 12.77 m/s threshold: damage, "eventually", whenever the drift lined up with a landing), and at 30 m/s elytra speed each such pair read as a +-60 m/s acceleration spike, ramping the smoothed g-force into blindness and nausea on a perfectly straight glide - and again a few seconds later as the clocks realigned. Players and player-driven vehicles are now measured PER CLIENT TICK: every applied move packet is one sample, the client's own per-tick velocity report (sent ahead of each move packet) is its clock, and the physics steps once per sample - twice on a two-packet tick, not at all on an empty one (nothing moved, nothing was felt, no impact is looked for).
- Impacts against Valkyrien Skies ship blocks are detected again. Ship hits come back from the raycast addressed in shipyard coordinates, and the seam-graze cross-section test (added with the slime-wall fixes) compared them against the entity's WORLD-space swept box — which never overlaps a shipyard cell — so every ship impact was thrown away as a graze. The test now runs in the ship's own space (the swept box through the ship's world-to-ship transform); the floor-ahead rule follows it, and the debug rays show ship hits in red again.
- Walking against sand walls no longer periodically snaps the player and pulses the g-force overlay. Block passability read the entity's LIVE deltaMovement at shape-query time, so any one-tick speed spike — from any source — could briefly turn adjacent sand passable: the walking player wedged slightly into the wall, vanilla snapped them back out, and the cycle repeated about once a second, feeding the g-force effects. Phasing now requires TWO ticks of evidence: the instantaneous speed past the threshold AND the previous tick's measured (position-based) speed confirming it at 80% — a genuine dive or flight always satisfies both, a spike never does. Passability and engulf-drag share the same confirmed gate so they can never disagree, and every phase activation for a player is logged (rate-limited) with the speeds that triggered it.
- Jumping the instant you land no longer deals phantom impact damage. The stopping force compares each axis against the last two ticks, and a perfectly timed jump sign-flipped against the landing's downward remnant — matching the "bounce" rule and billing fall speed PLUS jump speed as one huge vertical stop. An upward velocity born on the ground is now recognized as self-powered (the ground can only stop a fall to zero, never fling you upward): the landing is measured against zero instead. Slime and bed bounces are unaffected (they apply pre-contact, mid-air), and jump-into-ceiling impacts still hurt (those are measured on the stop tick).
- Entering and leaving a moving boat now reliably transfers momentum. The one-shot handoff kept losing to late vanilla sync on the client — dismounting makes the server send an absolute position packet whose handler overwrites the player's motion, and boarding has equivalent transient overwrites. The momentum is now captured client-side from the vehicle's locally observed movement and re-applied for a few consecutive ticks on the owning side, outliving any one-shot overwrite.
- When a driven boat ricochets off slime, the rider's head now turns WITH the boat (same yaw delta), so you keep facing the same way relative to the hull instead of staring backwards over the stern — and it eases around smoothly through the same gradual camera correction the on-foot bounce uses, rather than snapping.
- Dismounting a moving boat no longer freezes the boat: while ridden, the server's copy zeroed its own motion every tick, so handing simulation back to the server on dismount resumed from zero. The vehicle is reseeded with its real velocity at the handoff — both the ejected rider AND the boat keep their momentum.
- The dismount momentum seed now actually reaches the boat: it read the vehicle's movement as position-minus-old-position, which is only nonzero between a position update and the entity's next tick — and a shift-dismount is processed after the ridden boat's tick, exactly where that difference reads zero, so the seed silently failed and the boat stopped regardless. The velocity now comes from the capability's per-tick measurement (valid at any point in the tick) or the position delta, whichever is live.
- Shallow-angle slime hits bounce OUT along the wall instead of backwards. A shallow approach genuinely sweeps into the wall column, so the collision rays could graze the INTERNAL seams between the wall's blocks — faces whose normals oppose the tangential travel at full speed — and the bounce picked such a seam as "most opposed", mirroring the entity backwards (then back and forth until the speed died). A face buried against a neighboring block is now never a collision surface, in the classifier and the debug rays alike.
- The thin-air bubble HUD no longer flickers, and Respiration works correctly at altitude. The Respiration roll skipped the entire drain including the +4 that compensates vanilla's out-of-water air regen, so air bounced up and down every few ticks (constant pop-animation flicker) — and with Respiration III, 3 of 4 ticks spared, air ROSE on net and the bubbles never went down. Respiration now spares only the extra drain: spared ticks hold air exactly level, like Respiration underwater.
- One impact cracks a block ONCE. A single hit is detected on several consecutive ticks (contact tick plus the stale pre-impact window), and while the damage pipeline dedups that, the crack accumulator did not — an anchoring grappling hook re-billed its full crack fraction ~3 times per throw and could break through on the echo. Crack/break interactions on a block within 8 ticks of its last recorded hit now count as the same impact.
- Player-caused kinetic block breaking works with mobGriefing off: a player's own fired projectile (grappling hook) and the vehicle they drive now count as the player acting on the world, matching vanilla's convention for player-lit TNT. Mob-thrown projectiles and mob-driven vehicles stay gated by mobGriefing.
- Summoned/spawned mobs take wall damage now: every entity JOINING the level was blanket-marked as teleported — 5 ticks of damage immunity with velocity resets — so a mob summoned with Motion crossed its first ~10 blocks untracked and hit walls for free (falls worked only because they outlast the window). The teleport shield now only applies to entities whose tracking was already running (genuine re-add position jumps).
- Rapid consecutive bounces work: the 6-tick bounce refractory (which predates the seam guard and moving-away check that now prevent the pinning it guarded against) blocked legitimate double-bounces — a projectile ricocheting between two slime walls, or a player in a tight slime corridor. Reduced to a same-tick dedup.
- Tagged physics projectiles get a mass floor (half a block) for block breaking: mass is derived from hitbox volume, and a tiny hook's kinetic energy couldn't crack ice at ANY speed — even big entityWeights multipliers barely registered. A dense metal hook now smashes by speed, not hitbox size; entityWeights still raises it further.
- Projectiles can hit floor blocks: the "ignore floors unless falling fast" walking-artifact rule also blinded fired projectiles arcing down into floor ice; they are exempt now (near-flat grazes are still filtered by the approach-speed gate).
- Newly spawned entities are tracked from their very first tick. The velocity history started empty, so fast-spawning entities (projectiles above all) read a pre-impact speed of zero on tick 1 — the dispatcher skipped them — and anything spawning over 60 m/s also tripped the teleport-discontinuity guard. A tagged grappling hook fired at nearby ice hit it before FullStop processed a single tick of its flight. The history is now seeded from the spawn motion.
- Running fast on sand no longer sinks, jitters, and blacks you out. Above the phase speed, phaseable blocks lost their collision shape INCLUDING the sand underfoot — the floor vanished, you sank, engulf-drag slowed you below the threshold, the sand re-solidified around your legs, and vanilla shoved you back out, over and over (also smashing the sand and ramping g-force). Blocks whose top is at or below your feet now stay solid unless you're genuinely diving (falling faster than 8 m/s); dune walls and canopies you fly into phase exactly as before.
- Accumulated block cracks are now saved with the world. The crack state lived only in server RAM, so it could survive or die independently of the world across a relog — blocks looked cracked but accumulated from scratch when hit. Crack data is now level SavedData: after a relog the cracks are restored both visually and functionally, and unloaded chunks keep their cracks untouched.
- Collision particles now spawn at the actual impact point on the block face (the ray hit location) instead of at the entity's center.
- Landing on the corner of stairs no longer deals phantom damage (sometimes lethal from a two-block hop). On corner geometry, vanilla auto-step pops the entity upward by up to 12 m/s on a tick where onGround reads false, so the spike entered the velocity history as real motion — and sign-flipped against the next falling tick into a huge fake "bounce" stopping force, repeatedly. Near-ground upward motion at self-power scale (jump/step) is now never treated as momentum.
- Boats, minecarts, and falling blocks can now actually damage what they ram. The entire target-damage loop was gated behind `instanceof LivingEntity` for the mover — a fast boat hitting a standing player dealt nothing (a MOVING player only got hurt because their own victim-side pass billed them, which a standing player never runs).
- Death messages now name non-living attackers: killed by a boat reads "was hit by Boat" / "slammed into Boat" instead of the generic "experienced kinetic energy" (the damage-source builders only accepted living entities).
- Debug rays now show flush wall contact (yellow) symmetrically. floor() handedness meant a ray corner exactly on a block boundary traversed the +axis cell — rubbing a NORTH/WEST wall ran the rays in the air column and showed green while touching, while SOUTH/EAST showed yellow. Ray starts are now nudged outward by half the seam-guard margin, so both sides read identically (and the sliver can never turn a rub into a collision).
- Re-entering a boat that had coasted to a stop no longer launches it with its old speed: the boat's client-side velocity measurement is only taken while you drive it, so it froze at the fast pre-dismount value and the boarding-momentum seed "restored" it to a standing boat. Measurements are now only trusted when fresh (taken within the last two ticks).
- Vanilla entity pushing is suppressed between an ex-rider and their ex-vehicle during the dismount grace. Vanilla push applies a purely lateral shove between overlapping entities — irrelevant when dismounted boats always stopped, but with dismount momentum the pair travels side by side for many ticks and the accumulated push visibly veered the boat off left/right.
- The freshly-dismounted rider and their vehicle no longer "collide" with each other: vanilla parks the player right beside the still-moving boat, and the entity-collision momentum exchange dead-stopped the boat while launching the player. The pair is now exempt from entity collisions for the dismount grace period (2 s), in both directions.
- Entity collisions now read a player's MEASURED velocity instead of their server-side deltaMovement (which is ~always zero — player movement is client-authoritative). Every player used to register as a stationary wall: anything that grazed one dumped its full momentum into the "impact".
- Player-driven vehicle physics moved to the driver's client — the architectural fix for the whole family of ridden-boat bugs. A driven vehicle is client-authoritative: the server sees its positions only after they are clipped, detects wall contact a tick late, and everything it wrote back (bounces, skips, momentum) raced the client's own simulation — hence weak bounces, bounce-then-dead-stop, and lost momentum no matter how the sync was timed. Bounces and water skips for the vehicle you drive now run on your client (`ClientVehiclePhysics`), with fresh positions and pre-contact detection, exactly like on-foot physics; the server handles unmanned and mob-driven vehicles as before, and keeps ownership of damage, sounds, and particles.
- Boats (and horses) now turn into the rebound when bouncing off slime — the vehicle ricochets and points where it's going, the riding player's head stays free.
- A passenger's camera no longer rotates on bounces: the vehicle takes the bounce; the rider's own rays grazing the surface must not yank their view around.
- Entering a moving boat now truly keeps its momentum: the velocity is read from the boat's locally observed movement and applied on the same side at the exact takeover moment — no packets, nothing to race.
- Dismounting a moving vehicle (or being thrown off a destroyed one) now hands the vehicle's momentum to the passenger instead of freezing them in place.
- All bounce paths (server bounce, water skip, bounce camera) use the pre-impact velocity window, the same convention the damage pipeline already had.
- Riding a boat across ice no longer breaks the ice under it. A seated passenger's hitbox floor sits 0.45 blocks below the boat's hull (vanilla riding offsets) — inside the floor layer — so the rider's collision rays travelled THROUGH the ice and read every block seam as a head-on wall at boat speed, feeding the block-breaking path. A passenger's collision floor is now the higher of their own box floor and their vehicle's: the hull is between their legs and the world. (Debug rays and the floor-vs-wall rule follow the same effective floor.)
- Entering a moving boat keeps its momentum. Boarding hands control to the driver's client, which starts from its stale remote motion (~0), so a boat gliding past dead-stopped the moment you hopped in. The boat's measured velocity is now re-applied to the new driver right after the mount syncs.
- Ground-level wall slams deal damage again. Vanilla multiplies a grounded entity's deltaMovement by ground friction (~0.55) after moving, so the velocity the client reported each tick was roughly HALF its real speed — and the server's plausibility window was just wide enough to accept that deflated value as a "refinement" every tick, halving the measured stopping force of every grounded impact. Airborne there is no ground friction, which is why the same slam hurt the moment your feet left the ground. The client report now only ever refines the measurement UPWARD (not-yet-integrated intent, e.g. a jump's first tick); a smaller value falls back to the server's own measurement. A second, physics-based evidence path also corroborates wall hits independently of the client's collision flags: an entity parked flush against a face it approached, with the speed on that axis gone, has provably hit it.
- The default horizontal damage threshold moved from 12.77 to 10.5 m/s. The old default sat exactly in the sprint-jump band (the jump adds a flat +4 m/s): Speed-boosted ground runs stayed under it while the same run plus a hop crossed it. 10.5 keeps every unboosted movement safe with margin (sprint-jump peaks at 9.6) while Speed V+ runs and real launches hurt. NOTE: existing config files keep their saved 12.77 — delete the `velocityDamageThresholdHorizontal` line (or set it to 10.5) to pick up the new default.
- Rubbing along a slime/honey wall at speed no longer randomly reads as a head-on collision (red debug rays, mirror bounces that spin you around, honey pulse-braking). Vanilla parks a sliding entity flush against the surface, so the collision rays ran exactly in the wall plane and clipped the seam face of the next block along the wall — a face whose normal opposes travel at your full speed. Hits now require the entity's swept volume to genuinely overlap the face's cross-section; rubbing overlaps by exactly zero, so it can never register at any speed. Applies to walls, floors, and ceilings alike, and the debug ray colors follow the same rule.
- Slime bounces now work while riding a boat (or any player-driven vehicle). Player-controlled vehicles are client-authoritative — the server-side boat zeroes its own motion every tick, so the bounce velocity written on the server never reached the boat. Bounces and water skips are now delivered to the driver's client directly (network protocol bumped to 3).
- Corner bounces no longer rotate the camera in the wrong direction: the camera aimed off whichever hit face the rays found first; it now picks the face most opposed to your motion, the same one the server bounces you off.
- Bounce camera pitch (looking up/down) only follows the rebound during elytra flight. Jumping into a slime wall on foot no longer yanks the view upward; yaw still turns to follow the bounce.
- Riding passengers are no longer independently bounced by their vehicle's impact (the written motion was ignored by vanilla anyway and burned the rider's bounce cooldown).
- A living entity without the Forge gravity attribute no longer crashes the physics tick (degrades to vanilla gravity).
- `maxDamagePercent` config description now matches what the code does (it caps the total buffed damage as a multiple of the original, not the bonus).

- Horizontal collision damage works again for players: the server can never see a player's `horizontalCollision` flag (move packets arrive pre-clipped), so the damage evidence gate rejected every wall/ceiling hit. The client now reports its collision flags in the velocity packet (network protocol bumped to 2). Wall and ceiling impacts — running, sprint-jumping, and elytra — now deal damage past the threshold.
- Fall damage is consistent again: a two-tick impact could land a small partial hit on the contact tick, which started the damage cooldown and swallowed the real hit one tick later (9/15/18/21-block falls dealing one heart). Damage now tops up to the larger value during the cooldown window.
- Standing flush against a wall no longer counts as a collision: block hits now require ~2 m/s of approach speed into the face. This removes the red debug rays while wall-hugging, the sound/particle spam when stopping beside a wall, and the honey-wall collision spam.
- Brushing along slime/honey walls no longer bounces or dead-stops the player: bounces now require a mostly-direct impact (or ≥8 m/s into the surface, so fast grazes still bounce).
- Boats no longer visually bounce off slime long before reaching it: vanilla boats interpolate over 10 ticks (other entities use 3), leaving the rendered boat several blocks behind its real position; boat interpolation now matches other entities.
- Entering a minecart (or any mount) from a distance no longer reads as a violent movement that breaks nearby blocks and kills the rider — mount/dismount position snaps are treated as teleports.
- Minecarts actually skip across water now (their skip threshold was above their vanilla top speed).
- Note blocks no longer replay several times per bump (per-block retrigger cooldown).
- Running into buttons now presses them: buttons have no collision shape, so the collision rays could never see one; a swept-volume pass handles them.
- Getting swallowed by sand/gravel is sticky again: vanilla's push-toward-open-space shove is suppressed while buried in engulfing blocks, so you must jump/dig your way out instead of being ejected (this also fixes flying into a sand cube pushing you back out).
- Fixed OpenAL "Invalid parameter" console spam while drowning: audio-filter AL calls were made from the game thread and raced the sound engine's own error checks; they now run on the sound thread.
- The g-force blackout no longer snaps to full black after a lag spike — it still activates immediately but fades in over a few frames.
- High-speed FOV stretch can no longer push the FOV past 180° and flip the camera upside down.
- Debug raycast view updates per frame again (interpolated with entity rendering), and rays are only red for collision-grade hits — touching without colliding is yellow.
- Block crack overlays appear much earlier (from 15% of break energy instead of 40%), so medium-speed impacts visibly crack walls.
- Button pressing is precise now: the sweep covers one tick of travel (no more activating a full block early), the entity must overlap the button's actual outline shape, and it must be moving into the button's face. Brushing along a wall no longer presses its buttons; floor buttons need something falling onto them.
- Ice (and other blocks) can no longer be cracked or broken by sliding across them: breaking requires real speed into the hit face, so a boat gliding on packed ice is harmless while head-on impacts are unchanged.
- A piston-launched boat with a rider no longer randomly dead-stops shortly after launch: the entity collision system was treating the boat's own passenger as a collision target (the passenger's velocity reads as zero) and the momentum exchange ate the boat's speed. An entity's own ride stack is now excluded from entity collisions.
- Fixed floor seams reading as walls: the bottom collision rays start 0.1 above the surface, and any tiny downward drift (boat gravity, micro-hops) dipped them into the next floor block's side face at every block boundary. That phantom "wall" hit at full horizontal speed caused repeated collision sounds while boating on ice, smashed the ice under a launched boat (breaking the boat with it), and dead-stopped ridden boats when the phantom damage synced the server's stale velocity to the client. Side faces on blocks whose top is at foot level are now classified as floor, and non-living grounded movers (boats, minecarts) get the same vertical drift cleanup living entities already had.

### Changed

- FullStop now requires **Camafy** (0.1.x), the shared library behind FullStop and WeightRS. The Valkyrien Skies and Gravity Unbound compat, the drowning/g-force audio muffle and the fall/bounce maths now come from it (same behavior; the muffle now combines with other mods' muffles, strongest wins, and FullStop no longer carries its own sound-engine mixins).
- Entity mass comes from Camafy's weight model, the same one WeightRS uses (tuned in `config/camafy-weight.toml`), instead of hitbox volume times the `entityWeights` multipliers — that server config key is gone. A player weighs exactly what it did, but other entities change relative to it: a cow is ~5x heavier than before (5.6 vs 1.13 player-units of 0.648), an iron golem lighter (26 vs 42), a skeleton heavier (0.39 vs 0.24), a ghast much lighter (0.26 vs 8.0), a horse a little heavier (3.9 vs 3.1); unlisted modded mobs are ~1.3x their hitbox volume. Mass no longer changes with pose: crouching (+20%) and swimming or crawling (3x) players weigh the same as standing ones, and the elytra 3x rule (which only undid the smaller gliding hitbox) is gone. Only an entity's body counts, never what it carries.
- The sonic boom is now the CROSSING of the sound barrier, not an interval: one boom fires on the subsonic-to-supersonic transition, then nothing for the rest of the supersonic flight until the entity genuinely drops below the barrier (with hysteresis, so speed jitter at 343 m/s can't machine-gun booms) and breaks it again.
- The sonic boom is a moving pressure wave: the flyer booms once at the barrier crossing, and every player the supersonic entity passes afterward is swept by the shock cone exactly once — heard at their own position, louder the closer the flyby — until the entity drops subsonic and breaks the barrier again. Costs one distance check per player per tick only while something is supersonic.
- Overloaded stacks crush their carriers: every mob in a ride stack evaluates the mass riding above it, and past ~1.5x its own mass the load deals periodic squish damage — the bottom of a tall tower suffers most, so overbuilt stacks shed from the bottom up. A single similar-sized rider stays safe.
- Mobs mounted through the land-on-a-mob feature now behave like startled animals instead of statues: they PANIC when first landed on (vanilla panic goal — collision mounts only, saddled riding is untouched), they keep wandering while ridden as long as nobody is steering them (slower under load: speed scales with the carrier's share of the stack's mass, so one player on a cow is ~0.6x and taller stacks crawl), and loaded stacks ride low in water — the passengers' weight pushes the swimmer down proportionally, so a cow stacked on a cow submerges it.

- Ice toughness is tiered, with real cracks: the ice family (`fullstop:crackable` tag — ice, packed ice, blue ice) is out of the insta-break fragile tag and resists impacts at its real hardness. Every solid sub-break impact adds PERSISTENT damage to the block, shown as the vanilla mining-crack overlay (visible to everyone, per block, not per entity), and cracked blocks need less energy to finish — so pristine ice is tough while cracked ice shatters from the next good hit. Cracks heal and fade after ~30 s without further hits. Glass and snow stay insta-fragile.

### Added

- **`fullstop:physics_blacklist` entity-type tag** (empty by default): entities FullStop ignores completely — no tracking, collisions, bounces, or damage. For modded entities that manage their own physics.
- **`fullstop:physics_projectiles` entity-type tag** (empty by default): opt modded projectiles (grappling hooks and the like) into FullStop's FULL physics — slime/bed bounces, water skips, kinetic block cracking and breaking (a fast hook shatters glass), and entity collision damage — all applied by the pre-contact lookahead before the projectile's own impact handling fires. Tune how hard a light projectile hits with the entityWeights config (mass drives block breaking).
- **`crackHealSeconds` server config** (default 0): accumulated impact cracks are now PERMANENT by default — they persist and save with the world until the block breaks or is replaced. Set a number of seconds to restore timed healing.
- **Falling sticky blocks cling mid-fall**: a falling slime/honey block that passes beside a solid block grabs onto it instead of falling past — the same any-face support rule placed sticky blocks already follow.
- **Better Breathing**: Respiration is renamed Better Breathing (it already slows thin-air suffocation at altitude the same way it slows drowning — the name now says so).
- **Phase-through effects**: crashing through `fullstop:phaseable` blocks (leaves) now rustles — block particles and the block's quiet hit sound while you pass through.

- **`fallDamageMode` server config** — `KINETIC` (default, FullStop's stopping-force model) or `VANILLA_PARITY`: impacts convert to the vanilla fall distance that produces the same impact speed and deal exactly vanilla fall damage (a 20-block fall onto grass hurts like vanilla; Jump Boost included, dripstone matches vanilla's ×2). Wall/ceiling crashes use the same conversion. Feather Falling, armor, belly flops, and soft-block tags still apply in both modes.
- **`kineticDamageMultiplier` server config** — global scale for all kinetic impact damage (default 1.0).
- **`hardnessAffectsDamage` server config** — turn off the block-hardness damage scaling without leaving KINETIC mode (default true).
- **`minimumSolidImpactDamage` server config** — the previously hardcoded 1-heart floor on over-threshold solid impacts is now configurable (0 disables it).
- **Potion of Acclimation** (Awkward + Phantom Membrane; extendable with Redstone): the high-altitude counterpart to Water Breathing — no air loss in thin air while active.
- **Lung Capacity** attribute (`fullstop:lung_capacity`, default 1.0): divides the thin-air drain rate; a base for how long breath lasts.
- Thin-air air loss is now ramped and capped: entering thin air pops bubbles slowly at first, faster the higher/longer you stay, up to a hard cap — even teleporting thousands of blocks up leaves several seconds before suffocation starts.
- `fullstop:slowing` block tag (honey + the soul sand family): moving beside these blocks applies a continuous, silent slow — soul sand now slows like honey does, without collision sound/particle spam.
- Client config `rotateCameraOnlyWhenFlying`: restrict bounce camera rotation to elytra flight.
- Per-interaction server config toggles: `kineticDoorOpening`, `kineticButtonPressing`, `kineticNoteBlocks`, `kineticBellRinging`, and `sandBlasting` (all default on).

## 2.0.0 — Minecraft 1.20.1 (Forge)

A full rework of the mod on a new codebase.

### Data-Driven Block Behaviors

Block physics and behaviors are now defined by block tags under `data/fullstop/tags/blocks/`, so datapacks and other mods can customize them without code changes:

- `fullstop:fragile` — blocks that shatter easily and count as nearly zero hardness on impact (glass, ice, snow).
- `fullstop:soft_landing` — blocks that absorb most of an impact (hay).
- `fullstop:cushioning` — blocks that soften an impact (wool, leaves, moss, ...).
- `fullstop:phaseable` — blocks that fast-moving entities pass through instead of colliding with (leaves).
- `fullstop:engulfing` — phaseable blocks that swallow fast movers (sand, gravel, snow): heavy drag bleeds off their speed, and once slowed they become lodged inside and must dig their way out.
- `fullstop:gravity_affected` — blocks that fall like sand when unsupported.
- `fullstop:sticky` — gravity-affected blocks that cling to any touching block (slime, honey), so they only fall when floating completely free.
- `fullstop:kinetic_immune` — entity types that never take kinetic damage (bosses, flying/agile/soft-bodied mobs).

### Unsupported (Gravity-Affected) Blocks

- Added a data-driven unsupported block system: blocks tagged `fullstop:gravity_affected` become falling blocks when no longer connected to the world. Columns collapse one block at a time, just like sand. This system will be expanded in future updates.
- Slime and honey blocks now fall when unsupported. As `fullstop:sticky` blocks, they count a neighbor on any face as support, so they can still hang from walls and ceilings.

### New Block Interactions

- Entities colliding with engulfing blocks (sand, gravel, snow) at high speed become lodged inside and must dig their way out.
- Fast-moving entities phase through passable blocks such as leaves instead of taking impact damage.
- Kinetic block breaking: fragile blocks shatter on high-speed impact.

### G-Force & Blackouts

- Expanded G-force and blackout mechanics with new status effects tied to sustained G-force.
- Revamped G-force visuals and sounds, with smooth eased blackout effects.
- Blackouts can now occur while drowning (blackout extended to air supply).
- Blackout effects now render correctly in third person.
- Added new enchantments and status effects.

### Environmental Damage

- Added atmospheric damage.
- Added underwater pressure damage.

### Physics & Performance

- Improved physics calculations, including reworked collision detection and bounce edge cases.
- Major server-tick optimization of the physics loop:
  - Collision raycasts are skipped entirely when the volume the entity sweeps through this tick is
    provably empty (open air, flat ground) — the common case for almost every entity.
  - When rays do fire away from Valkyrien Skies ships, they use a vanilla-equivalent fast path that
    bypasses ship-aware raycast wrappers and reuses a single collision context (ship impacts still
    use the ship-aware path when a ship is nearby).
  - Capability lookups are cached per entity (field read instead of a Forge dispatcher walk).
  - Per-tick synched-data reads (NoAI checks) are cached and refreshed once a second.
  - Items, XP orbs, and area-effect clouds are excluded from physics before any work is done.
- Tweaked the sonic boom cooldown.

### Configuration

- Added new configuration options, including `enableGravityBlocks` to toggle the unsupported block system.
